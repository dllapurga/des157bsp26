Project Overview

My capstone project is an interactive website that serves as a virtual recipe archive, allowing my kids, nieces, nephews, and future decendenmts to preserve and view the recipes passed down through generations within the family. At its core, the project explores how recipes and stories function as a living connection to culture, memory, and cultural identity. Through images, videos, and recipes, it's my hope to create a connection between the younger generations in my family with the elders they may never get the chance to meet.

As the child and grandchild of immigrants in America, I've grown up with access to the flavors and dishes that shaped my family’s history. When I crave these foods, there are still relatives who know how to prepare them. But I often think about future generations of my family—third, fourth, or even fifth generation descendants—who may no longer have that cultural connection. While cultural foods can still be found in restaurants, they can never fully replicate the method, flavor, intimacy, history, and care embedded in recipes that have been prepared within a family for generations.

Food is one of the most enduring expressions of cultural identity, yet recipes are fragile forms of knowledge. Many are never formally written down and instead survive through memory, routine, and shared experience. As generations pass, these culinary traditions risk disappearing, taking with them the stories, emotions, and histories tied to them. This project aims to preserve those recipes before they are lost to time, creating a digital space where future generations can reconnect with the flavors, memories, and cultural legacies of their families.

Project Research

Through comparative analysis, I realized that there were little to none websites targeted towards preserving a set cultural recipes or even direct family recipes. While their were recipe websites with different recipes organized in a grid, they didn't have much of a narrative or story tied them. They were just recipes, but wholesome stories and memories weren't being preserved and shared through them. I wanted to convey these recipes as more than mere steps. They're capsules of memory that shouldn't simply be lost to time. In many, you click on the recipe in the grid and just scroll to see the recipe and ingredients. In a lot of them, the UI even felt outdated or too common.

I wanted my whle project feel like a kitchen or dining experience. That's why rather than a typical scroll, I made the lazy-susan the method in which the user cycles through recipes. I added images, videos, and even written stories to make the project feel warmer and more personal. From the websites I observed, I didn't want it to be a collection of food. Food is everywhere. We encouter everyday and it alone is nothing special. It's the stories associated with food of people gathered around a table, it's the laughs and deep conversations that take place over a meal, its the stories of reunion and loss that give it its significance. Every person's story is different. The siginficance of different meals is different from person to person and these unique wholesome experiences are what deserve be given a platform to shine.

Visual Design

The visual design is a reflection of my personal cultural background as well as my own personal life story. The background graphic was inspired by the tile in my grandmother's kitchen back when I used to cook with her a lot throughout my childhood. The lazy-susan design was inspired by dim sum culture from my Cantonse background. The individual images of the different dishes were taken from an aerial view to create the visual of the dishes being placed on top of the table.

The ripped pieces of paper presenting the recipes, ingredients, and images are not only to give a tangible and scrapbook-like feel to this virtual experience, but to also represent the way my grandmother would rip pieces of paper off of scrap paper lying around to jot quick things down— one of these things being quick recipe notes. Through the choice of color, I wanted to convey a sense of nostalgia, but remain modern, clean, and minimal. For the typeface, I wanted it to remain clearly legible, hence the use of Jost, but I walso wanted it to be look handwritten like how my grandparents' recipes were.

User Interface

I added the cover screen to provide the user with some context as to who the target audience is and what the purpose of the project is. Once scrolling up as indicated by the chevron arrows, the cover page pulls up out of the screen like a peeling piece of paper to represent the peeling action of flipping the page of a cook book. The lazy susan table is a clickable image of a rotating table where clicking the right half rotates it clockwise and clicking the left half rotates it counterclockwise. Each rotation cycles through four recipes and swaps out the recipe title, recipe card image, step-by-step image, character cutout, explanation image, and video. A small text hint also helps instruct the user to click to rotate.

The memory wall is a community input section at the bottom of the project. Users are prompted to share a food memory and submit. Submitted memories are saved to a database so that they are able to remain across sessions and are visible to all visitors. Each memory comes out as a card in a responsive grid with a delete option just in case the user would like to change their mind.

I then added an animate on scroll library to the project. I liked the fading animation of the elements within the project because to me, they represented the gentle fading in of memories as the user scrolls through the different recipes. It's also representative of the fleeting of memories as time moves on. This fleeting of time is why the project chooses to preserve the memory.

Revisions

I first started this project intending for the user to fill out a multi-step form where they fill in the recipe themselves with the name of the dish, the ingredinets, the steps, and the image. This data that they input themselves through the multi-step form will then be input on the respective recipe page. The user clicks the lazy-susan, the site will cycle through all the recipes and the respective contents will swap in and out.

However, due to time-constraints and my current proficiency in coding, in the process, I realized that I wouldn't have much time to complete the project, and that considering my ability js, there were some features that I didn't know how to do and had to rely on too many outside resources. Due to this, I adjusted my project so that instead of the user adding in the recipes, ingredients, dish name, and photo, I added all that already so that the user simply views the already-made archive instead of making it themselves. To now compensate for the last of interactivity, I added a textbox to the bottom of the page to allow the user to reflect on their own life and share a memory of their own. Moving forward, I just had to adjust the spacing to allow for some negative space to make the site more breathable and to increase font size to make it more accessible.

Summary

Working on this project, I learned a lot about how to create a narrative through interactive media and how to embed said narrative within the project experience itself. I was able to practice more with back4app for storage and I was able to learn more on how to use libraries.

I'm most proud of the wholesome narrative I was able to express to this interactive media.I truly feel like I'm preserving stories that otherwise would be lost to time, and as a project directed to descendents in the family after me, I like the idea of the younger generations being able to meet their ancestors a lot. For a lot of us now, our ancestors never had photos or videos, so now that these technologies exist, I think it's a beautiful thing to introduce them through the main action they used to express their love: cooking.

The most rewarding part was getting the lazy-susan animation to work. Being able to cycle though recipes this way was my primay challenge after deciding to scrap the multi-step form, but after recieving help, I was able to complete the code and get the feature to function fully. Now that it works, I'm very proud of myself for coming up with that concept to cycle through recipes.
